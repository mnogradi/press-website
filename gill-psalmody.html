John Gill’s Forgotten Sermon on Exclusive Psalmody

By Morgan Nogradi

October 19, 2026 • 4 min read

Most people know John Gill for his Exposition of the Old and New Testaments. This is rather like knowing Daniel Defoe for Robinson Crusoe.

Technically correct.

But incomplete.

In 1734, Gill published a sermon called A Discourse on Singing of Psalms. Preached on December 25, 1733, to a society of young men meeting for prayer at a meeting-house on Horsley-down in Southwark, it dealt with a question that had occupied Particular Baptists for decades: what should Christians sing in public worship?

An Old Baptist Dispute

Benjamin Keach, Gill’s predecessor at Horsley-down, was among the early Particular Baptists to introduce congregational singing into his church. The practice met considerable resistance, and its acceptance did not settle every question. Before long, the debate had turned to what congregations ought to sing.

The question persisted into Gill’s ministry. According to Dr. George Ella, the minutes of Gill’s own church record the congregation changing its practice between psalms and hymns over the years. Apparently, the matter was not settled quite as neatly as one might hope.

Gill addressed the subject in his 1733 sermon, published the following year. The title is straightforward enough, but the work itself is easy to overlook beside his much better-known commentary.

For anyone curious about Gill’s views on psalmody, it offers a useful place to begin—without having to navigate all nine volumes of the Exposition.

The original edition is available to read through the Internet Archive:

John Gill, A Discourse on Singing of Psalms (1734)

Gill had plenty to say beyond his commentary. This is one place to find it.
